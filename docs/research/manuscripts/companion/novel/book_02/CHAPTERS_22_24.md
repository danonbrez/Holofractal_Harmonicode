authority,

date,

predecessor,

known successor.

"You're building an archive."

### Chapter Twenty-Three — Consensus Beta

Consensus Beta worked.

This was the worst possible beginning.

The pilot launched in three Bellwether service centers.

Residents could create one verified identity and use it across selected programs.

Proof of address reused.

Income documentation reused where authorized.

Benefits applications prefilled.

Permit status unified.

Fraud checks ran automatically.

Public notices personalized by neighborhood and service eligibility.

Wait times fell by nearly half.

Duplicate-document submissions collapsed.

Staff loved not retyping addresses.

Residents loved not bringing the same birth certificate to three offices.

Managers loved dashboards.

The mayor loved cameras.

Vane came to opening day.

Julian stood near the back with Ana.

"You're smiling," Ana said.

"It's good."

"You're disappointed."

"Deeply."

She laughed.

Vane demonstrated a resident applying for a childcare subsidy.

Identity verified.

Income attribute already validated through another program.

Address current.

No need to reupload.

The application completed in seven minutes.

The audience applauded.

Julian applauded too.

That mattered.

Consensus solved real friction.

A woman near him whispered, "Why didn't we always do this?"

Because the systems had grown separately.

Because laws differed.

Because data formats differed.

Because institutions distrusted one another.

Because boundaries had once been visible through inconvenience.

Integration removed the inconvenience.

It also removed some friction that had prevented quiet reuse.

The pilot included a consent dashboard.
