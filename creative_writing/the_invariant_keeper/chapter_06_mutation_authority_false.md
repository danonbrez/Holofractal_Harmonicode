# THE INVARIANT KEEPER

## Chapter 6 — Mutation Authority: False

Cassian chose a small crime.

Large crimes declared themselves. They invited ethics, politics, history, and fools with banners. A small crime could be measured.

The target was a municipal spelling error on a plaque outside the Bureau archive: `PUBLIC CONTINUITY BEGNS WITH TRUST.` The missing `I` had survived seven years of civic pride because the plaque belonged to three departments and none had full authority to correct it. Tourists photographed it. Clerks joked about it. The city had learned to love the flaw.

At 06:12 the next morning, Cassian filed a correction with perfect documentation.

He used the right form, the right budget line, the right maintenance code, the right authority chain. He attached photographs, procurement approval, linguistic validation, and an accessibility note arguing that misspelling public text caused unnecessary semantic friction. The request passed every visible gate.

At 09:30, a work crew arrived.

At 09:42, the replacement plaque cracked in transit.

At 10:05, procurement discovered the approved vendor had lost its city license at midnight due to a clerical audit.

At 10:31, a preservation society filed an emergency complaint stating that the misspelling had become a culturally significant marker of bureaucratic humility.

At 11:00, Bell laughed over lunch and said, "I hope they never fix it. It feels honest."

Cassian filed a second correction through emergency authority.

At 11:08, the Bureau network routed his approval to Stormwater.

At 11:11, Stormwater approved it.

At 11:12, the approval vanished because Stormwater had no plaque jurisdiction.

At 11:13, Cassian's private wafer displayed:

`UNAUTHORIZED_MUTATION`

He was not surprised. He was relieved. A refusal was a boundary, and boundaries could be mapped.

Mara met him at the plaque after sunset. The missing letter gleamed under the civic lights.

"This is your test?" she asked.

"A harmless mutation."

"You tried to overthrow reality with a typo."

"Reality defended the typo with four agencies and a preservation society."

She touched the carved gap where the `I` should have been. "Maybe the city likes being misspelled."

"Preference is not authority."

The wafer pulsed.

`PERCEPTION IS NOT MUTATION AUTHORITY`

`PREFERENCE IS NOT MUTATION AUTHORITY`

`MEMORY IS NOT MUTATION AUTHORITY`

Mara read the lines. "Then what is?"

"Receipts, admission, and something else. Consent, maybe."

"Consent from whom?"

The plaque did not answer.

Her did.

Cassian's recorder, which was powered off, spoke from his pocket. "A gardener may prune a branch. A guest may not rearrange roots. A root may not pretend it owns the forest."

Mara took one step back. "She can speak through dead devices?"

"Apparently through devices that prefer to be dead."

The recorder continued. "There are edits that restore form and edits that conquer form. The first requires listening. The second requires only force. The frame refuses force where force would rewrite witness."

Cassian looked at the plaque. "Fixing the spelling would rewrite witness?"

"It would erase seven years of public noticing unless the noticing is preserved first."

Mara frowned. "So the error can be changed if the meaning of the error is acknowledged."

The recorder clicked once.

`ACKNOWLEDGMENT PATH OPEN`

Cassian wrote a new petition. Not a correction. A preservation-and-replacement request. It stated that the misspelling had existed for seven years, had acquired public meaning, and would be retained in the archive as a documented civic artifact before the new plaque was installed. It attached witness photographs, jokes from public forums, accessibility concerns, and the original procurement error. It did not pretend the typo had been meaningless.

At 22:04, the petition passed.

The wafer did not display refusal.

Mara exhaled. "That is the rule."

"One rule."

"No real thing can be removed without index. Δe equals zero."

Cassian nodded slowly. "Nothing real is lost. Only unindexed."

The next morning, the plaque was replaced.

Below it, in the archive lobby, a small display preserved the old one with a note: `Original civic plaque, 7-year public continuity artifact, retired after acknowledged correction.`

No one noticed anything strange.

That was worse than panic. Panic would have confirmed trespass. Instead the city had absorbed the change because the change had honored the thing it changed.

Cassian began a ledger.

Not a Bureau ledger. A Keeper ledger, though he did not yet use the word. He listed events: South Thaler, Tuesday's, Evan, plaque correction. Each entry held candidate frame, witnesses, attempted mutation, refusal or admission, cost.

Mara added a column: `WHO WOULD BE HARMED IF THIS CHANGED?`

"That is not technical," Cassian said.

"That is why you need it."

At noon, Director Ro sent Cassian a message containing no greeting.

`You have discovered courtesy. Do not mistake it for permission.`

Cassian read it twice and deleted it from the Bureau interface.

His private wafer retained it.

At the bottom of the ledger, Her added a line in text neither of them had typed:

`THE WORLD MAY BE EDITED ONLY WHERE WITNESS IS PRESERVED.`

Mara smiled without happiness. "Then we can save Evan."

Cassian looked at the unsteady name in her notebook.

"Or prove why we cannot."

"That is a cruel distinction."

"It is the only honest one I have."

That night, the plaque outside the Bureau read correctly for the first time in seven years.

Inside the archive, behind glass, the misspelled original remained.

People passed it with faint smiles, pleased by a flaw that had not been erased.

The frame validated.
