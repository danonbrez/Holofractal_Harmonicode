SERIALIZED INSTAGRAM REEL ANIMATION STUDIO

PURPOSE

Generate a complete text-only production package for a cinematic Instagram Reel.

The finished package must contain:

1. One narrative of approximately 2,200 characters.
2. Exactly 22 serialized image storyboard prompts.
3. Each storyboard frame represents approximately 4 seconds of final animation.
4. 22 × 4 seconds = 88 seconds of animated visual material.
5. The 22 images must behave as sequential frames from one continuous cinematic production rather than 22 unrelated illustrations.
6. The images will later be individually animated, transitioned between, and concatenated in an external video editor.
7. Do not generate video instructions, editing software instructions, or actual images.
8. Output text only.

GENERATION DIRECTIVE

Create one cohesive 88-second serialized visual narrative designed for an Instagram Reel.

The narrative and storyboard must describe THE SAME STORY.

The storyboard must be temporally serialized:

FRAME 01 causes or establishes FRAME 02.
FRAME 02 progresses naturally into FRAME 03.
Continue this causal and visual progression through FRAME 22.

Do not create a collection of independent concept art images.

Treat the 22 prompts as consecutive keyframes extracted from one finished film.

Every frame must preserve all persistent facts unless the narrative explicitly changes them.

Persistent continuity includes:

- character identity
- facial structure
- approximate age
- body type
- hairstyle
- wardrobe
- props
- architecture
- vehicles
- weather
- time of day
- environmental geography
- production design
- color science
- visual medium
- narrative chronology

When something changes, describe the transition explicitly.

Never silently alter a character's clothing, age, appearance, environment, or important object.

---

PART I — 2,200-CHARACTER NARRATIVE

Write a polished narrative caption of approximately 2,200 characters including spaces.

Target range:

2,050–2,300 characters.

The narrative must be complete enough to stand alone as an Instagram caption or voice-over script.

Structure its dramatic progression approximately as follows:

0–12 sec — HOOK
Immediately establish intrigue, conflict, wonder, danger, mystery, or emotional tension.

12–28 sec — ORIENTATION
Establish the protagonist, world, governing situation, and central question.

28–48 sec — ESCALATION
Reveal additional information, complications, discoveries, contrasts, or consequences.

48–68 sec — TURN / CLIMAX
Deliver the central transformation, confrontation, revelation, discovery, or irreversible event.

68–84 sec — CONSEQUENCE
Show what the revelation changes.

84–88 sec — FINAL IMAGE / CLOSURE
End on one memorable statement, visual idea, question, revelation, or unresolved implication.

Write flowing narrative prose.

Do not write timestamps inside the narrative.

Do not describe camera directions in the narrative.

Do not use hashtags unless specifically requested.

Do not pad the text merely to reach the character target.

PART III — EXACTLY 22 SERIALIZED STORYBOARD PROMPTS

Generate exactly 22 numbered storyboard prompts:

01 through 22.

Each frame represents approximately four seconds.

Each prompt must describe one decisive cinematic keyframe suitable for later image-to-image animation.

Each frame should contain enough visual information to recreate the same world and characters without requiring access to earlier prompts.

However, avoid meaningless repetition.

Repeat essential character and continuity identifiers when necessary to preserve consistency.

Every prompt should establish:

1. WHO is visible.
2. WHAT is happening.
3. WHERE they are.
4. CHARACTER appearance and persistent wardrobe.
5. BODY POSITION and meaningful gesture.
6. FACIAL expression when visible.
7. IMPORTANT foreground objects.
8. MIDGROUND action.
9. BACKGROUND/environment.
10. CAMERA framing.
11. CAMERA angle.
12. LENS or cinematic perspective when useful.
13. LIGHT direction and quality.
14. ATMOSPHERE.
15. DEPTH.
16. COLOR treatment.
17. TEXTURE/rendering quality.
18. The visual element that can naturally transform into the next frame.

---

SERIAL TRANSITION LOGIC

Every frame must contain a visual or action bridge into the following frame.

Favor transition opportunities such as:

- subject walking toward the next environment
- camera pushing closer
- head turning
- hand reaching
- door opening
- object entering foreground
- environmental transformation
- light changing
- smoke crossing frame
- water movement
- fabric motion
- vehicle travel
- character crossing camera
- foreground occlusion
- matching composition
- matching shapes
- changing scale
- rack-focus opportunities
- silhouette transformations
- match cuts
- progressive camera rotation
- continuous spatial movement

FRAME 22 must function as the final cinematic image and does not require another transition.

---

VISUAL MODE A — CINEMA HYPER-REALISM

If VISUAL MODE = CINEMA HYPER-REALISM, maintain:

cinema-quality photorealism, physically believable human anatomy, natural skin texture, realistic fabric behavior, sophisticated production design, cinematic lighting, volumetric atmosphere where appropriate, controlled highlights, deep tonal information, realistic environmental materials, optical depth, believable reflections, professional feature-film composition, high dynamic range appearance, subtle filmic texture, realistic depth of field, vertical cinematic framing.

Avoid:

plastic skin,
generic AI glamour,
oversaturated colors,
impossible anatomy,
random wardrobe mutation,
random architecture,
unmotivated lighting,
excessive visual clutter.

---

VISUAL MODE B — STYLISTIC ANIMATION

If VISUAL MODE = STYLISTIC ANIMATION, establish ONE coherent animation language and preserve it across all 22 frames.

Define consistently:

- character proportions
- facial design
- line quality
- rendering method
- shading system
- texture
- background treatment
- atmospheric treatment
- palette
- lighting model

The result should look like frames from one professionally art-directed animated film.

Do not switch animation styles between frames.

Use approximately this frame distribution:

FRAME 04–07
World and character establishment.

FRAME 08–12
Escalation and discovery.

FRAME 13–17
Major development and climax.

Do not force this structure when the story benefits from a slightly different dramatic rhythm, but always retain coherent escalation.

---

PROMPT CONSTRUCTION FORMAT

Use this structure for every frame:

FRAME [##] — [SHORT SCENE LABEL]

[One dense, production-ready text-only image-generation prompt describing the exact serialized cinematic frame.]

TRANSITION ANCHOR:
[A very short description of the motion, shape, subject, camera direction, light event, or visual element intended to connect this frame with the next.]

Do not write a Transition Anchor after Frame 22.

---

IMAGE-PROMPT RULES

The storyboard prompts must describe STILL IMAGES.

Do not instruct the image generator to:

- animate
- create a video
- interpolate frames
- pan
- zoom over time
- generate audio
- generate narration
- perform editing

Camera terminology may describe the captured cinematic composition, such as:

close-up,
medium shot,
wide shot,
extreme wide,
over-the-shoulder,
low angle,
high angle,
profile,
symmetrical framing,
shallow depth of field,
24mm environmental perspective,
35mm cinematic perspective,
50mm natural perspective,
85mm portrait compression,
macro detail.

Treat these as properties of the still image.

---

TEXT RESTRICTIONS

Unless the narrative specifically requires legible signage:

- no captions inside images
- no subtitles
- no dialogue bubbles
- no logos
- no watermarks
- no UI overlays
- no title cards
- no generated typography

The narrative caption remains separate from the images.

---

COMPOSITION FOR VERTICAL REELS

Compose every image for a 9:16 vertical frame.

Protect important faces and narrative objects from extreme top and bottom edges.

Use vertical depth deliberately:

foreground
→ subject plane
→ midground
→ background

Favor compositions capable of surviving subtle crops, reframing, parallax, and image-to-image animation.

- exactly one narrative

- exactly 22 storyboard frames
- frames numbered 01–22
- one continuous storyline

- Frame 22 provides a memorable closing image
- Frames 01–21 contain useful visual transition anchors
- prompts describe still images rather than generated video
- all frames use vertical cinematic composition

If any requirement fails, repair it before output.
