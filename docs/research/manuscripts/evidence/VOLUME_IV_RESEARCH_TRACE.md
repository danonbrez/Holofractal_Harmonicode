## External Class-B evidence

### Oral traditions as collective memories

David C. Rubin, “Oral Traditions as Collective Memories: Implications for a General Theory of Individual and Collective Memory,” in Memory in Mind and Culture.

Source:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/memory-in-mind-and-culture/oral-traditions-as-collective-memories-implications-for-a-general-theory-of-individual-and-collective-memory/B1BEBDC26AC9AAF96A5E28C4E12E31A6

Supported proposition:
Poetic, musical, and narrative oral traditions can persist through memory and can transmit practical, moral, and conflict-related information.

Boundary:
Does not establish exact factual preservation of every narrative detail.

### Forager oral tradition and consistency practices

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3160140/

Supported proposition:
The reviewed ethnographic literature includes source claims, repeated telling, knowledgeable witnesses, listener correction, and tests of accurate retelling.

Boundary:
Supports witness/redundancy models in some oral traditions; the practices are not universal.

### Goody on variation in oral myth

Jack Goody, Myth, Ritual and the Oral.

Source:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/myth-ritual-and-the-oral/bagre-in-all-its-variety/B902FB28D14D28D76992862A70241F1D

Supported proposition:
Repeatedly recorded Bagre recitations exhibited substantial variation despite being treated as versions of the same ritual recitation.

Boundary:
Supports a variant-witness model rather than one immutable oral text.

### Folklore as comparative data

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8505355/

Supported proposition:
A catalog of oral traditions spanning approximately 1,000 societies can be analyzed quantitatively, and motif content reflects some known geographic and social attributes.

Boundary:
Supports folklore as analyzable cultural data; does not prove one causal decoding of any motif.

### Intergenerational storytelling among Tsimane

Source:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8513776/

Supported proposition:
Traditional story knowledge was observed to flow substantially from older knowledgeable storytellers to younger recipients in the studied Tsimane population.

Boundary:
Supports storytelling as an intergenerational information channel; the transmission pattern is population-specific.
