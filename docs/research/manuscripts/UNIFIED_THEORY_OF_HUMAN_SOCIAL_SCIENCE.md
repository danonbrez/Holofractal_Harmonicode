### Formal Introduction

Human social science has traditionally been divided by method before it has been divided by subject.

Physics asks what changes and what remains invariant. Mathematics asks which transformations preserve identity. Engineering asks whether a structure survives contact with load. Computer science asks whether a transition can be represented, executed, audited, and reversed. These disciplines examine different surfaces of a common problem:

**How does organized structure persist through change?**

The present thesis begins from the proposition that human beings cannot be adequately modeled by reducing one descriptive layer into another. Chemical description does not exhaust psychological description. Psychological description does not eliminate biological constraint. Social description does not erase individual agency. Symbolic description does not automatically become meaningless because its variables are not identical to those of laboratory instrumentation.

A non-reductive human science therefore requires several simultaneous descriptions whose boundaries remain explicit.

The proposed architecture is not an argument that every layer possesses equal evidentiary status. It is an argument that distinct layers must not be collapsed merely because one is easier to measure.

Its governing discipline is **closure**.

A proposition enters the architecture only insofar as its identity can be preserved while its consequences are traced.

# VIII. AGAINST REDUCTIVE DESCRIPTION

Reduction becomes invalid when a description at one level is mistaken for the complete causal identity of a system operating across several levels.

Suppose fear correlates with a measurable neurochemical change.

The measurement is real.

It does not follow that the chemical variable exhausts the meaning, origin, interpretation, learned history, environmental trigger, strategic value, or behavioral consequence of fear.

Likewise, discovering that a computer operation produces a voltage pattern does not make the algorithm identical to a list of voltages.

The voltages instantiate the computation.

They do not replace its logical description.

This thesis therefore treats neurochemistry as an **instrument surface** of biological state.

The supplied architecture calls it a biochemical dashboard.

That formulation can be retained provided the distinction remains exact:

> biochemical variables may index, participate in, constrain, or embody psychological states without thereby proving that psychological organization is reducible to a single biochemical scalar.

The causal relation must be demonstrated rather than presumed in either direction.

# Chapter 5  
# Projection and Collective Hysteresis

Individual projection scales socially.

If a group rewards particular distortions, the cost of maintaining them falls.

A mutually reinforcing system can therefore stabilize a false interpretation even when no single participant independently possesses enough evidence to sustain it.

Let:

\[
P_i
\]

be the private interpretation of participant \(i\).

Let:

\[
R
\]

be the reinforcement supplied by the group.

Then persistence may become:

\[
P_i + R \rightarrow P_i'
\]

where:

\[
P_i' > P_i
\]

in confidence even though the underlying evidence has not increased.

The group has amplified certainty rather than information.

This phenomenon can be called **collective hysteresis** when a social interpretation persists after the conditions that originally generated it have changed.

The engineering analogue is a system whose output remains path-dependent after input reversal.

The social-scientific audit therefore asks two separate questions:

1. What evidence produced the belief?
2. What mechanism currently maintains the belief?

These are not necessarily the same.
