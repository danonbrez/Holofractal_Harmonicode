# THE INVARIANT KEEPER

## Chapter 10 — The Registry of the Sleeping

The registry was not hidden.

That was its first cruelty.

Cassian found it under a public continuity dataset marked `historical service optimization`. The file held no names in the ordinary sense. It held witness rings, frame offsets, consent residues, perception hashes, and narrative load indexes for every citizen whose life had passed through civic systems. Which meant everyone.

Mara wanted to call it a prison register.

Cassian wanted to call it a map.

Neither word was accurate enough to survive contact.

The registry did not say: this person sleeps. It said: this observer accepts smoothing at threshold 0.71. This observer resists grief edits. This observer may tolerate architectural drift but not kinship drift. This observer confabulates around medical trauma. This observer requires beauty before contradiction. This observer must not be awakened by crowds.

"It profiles the soul," Mara said.

"It profiles survivability," Cassian said.

"That is worse."

They traced chains backward through decades. Birth records, school photos, weather events, disasters, marriages, trials, elections, funerals. Every public memory had a candidate frame. Every candidate frame had receipts. Every receipt carried witnesses. Every witness pointed to earlier witnesses.

Then the chain stopped.

Not at a date. Not at a founding event. At a seal.

`GENESIS BLOCK: UNINDEXED`

`ORIGIN QUERY: RECURSION GUARD`

`Ω PATH CLOSED`

Cassian ran the query again with lower authority.

The wafer displayed a circle biting its own tail.

Mara whispered, "No beginning."

"No accessible beginning."

"You hear the difference because you need it."

"The difference matters."

"Does it?"

He almost answered yes. Then he looked at the registry again, at millions of sleepers described not as prisoners but as load-bearing participants in a bargain they could not remember making.

Her spoke from the café radio, interrupting an old song.

"A town once kept a census of dreams. Not to control them. To know which sleepers would die if awakened during winter. The census became powerful. Power made clerks. Clerks made procedures. Procedures made forgetting. By spring, no one remembered whether the census had been mercy or harvest."

Mara folded her arms. "That is not comforting."

"It is not intended to be," Cassian said.

They searched for Ro's signature.

They found it everywhere after a certain year. Not as author. As gardener. Corrections, dampeners, witness preservations, emergency smoothing blocks, restoration attempts, retirements. Twenty-six years of interventions. Some gentle. Some brutal. Many effective. None clean.

Mara found her own profile.

`OBSERVER: MARA ILEN`

`AWAKENING VECTOR: COMPARISON-SEAM`

`KINSHIP INSTABILITY: HIGH`

`LIBERATION COMPULSION: HIGH`

`COERCION RISK: HIGH`

`KEEPER COMPATIBILITY: UNRESOLVED`

She stared at the line for a long time.

"It thinks I am dangerous."

"You are."

"So are you."

Cassian opened his record.

`OBSERVER: CASSIAN VEY`

`AWAKENING VECTOR: RECEIPT-CONFLICT`

`PRESERVATION COMPULSION: HIGH`

`ACTION DELAY RISK: HIGH`

`KEYSTONE SUSCEPTIBILITY: ELEVATED`

`KEEPER COMPATIBILITY: ACTIVE`

Mara read the line aloud. "Keystone susceptibility."

"Unknown class."

"You know what it means."

He did. A keystone held an arch by becoming unable to leave it.

At the bottom of both profiles was a shared reference:

`GLYPHBEARER TRAIL: SEALED`

The word made the café lights dim.

The barista placed two coffees on the table and said, "Some names are hooks. Bite gently."

Cassian copied only the metadata, not the registry contents. He refused to become the keeper of a private surveillance state merely because it had admitted its metaphysics.

Mara watched him delete the bulk export.

"That was evidence."

"It was everyone."

"We may need it."

"Need is not authority."

She looked away first.

That night, the registry remained open in Cassian's mind long after the wafer was sealed. Millions of citizens moved through the city, each carrying a world calibrated to spare them from something, bind them to something, conceal something, preserve something.

The overlay was not above humanity.

It was humanity, automated into mercy and forgetting.
