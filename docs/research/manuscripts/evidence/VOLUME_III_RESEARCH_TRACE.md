## External Class-B research

### Gelfand et al. — cultural tightness and looseness

Citation:

Michele J. Gelfand et al., “Differences Between Tight and Loose Cultures: A 33-Nation Study,” *Science* 332, no. 6033 (2011): 1100–1104.

DOI:

`10.1126/science.1197754`

Verified source:

`https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21617077/`

Supported proposition:

Cultures vary in norm strength and tolerance of deviance; the reported model associates cultural tightness with ecological and historical threat variables.

Bounded use:

Supports a threat-calibration hypothesis. Does not establish that any specific restrictive norm is adaptive.

### Centola — network diffusion of behavior

Citation:

Damon Centola, “The Spread of Behavior in an Online Social Network Experiment,” *Science* 329, no. 5996 (2010): 1194–1197.

DOI:

`10.1126/science.1185231`

Verified source:

`https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20813952/`

Supported proposition:

Behavioral adoption can depend on network structure and reinforcing exposures.

Bounded use:

Supports network-dependent contagion formalization. Does not imply that every cultural or moral behavior follows the same diffusion function.

### Fehr and Gächter — punishment and cooperation

Citation:

Ernst Fehr and Simon Gächter, “Altruistic Punishment in Humans,” *Nature* 415 (2002): 137–140.

DOI:

`10.1038/415137a`

Verified sources:

- `https://www.nature.com/articles/415137a`
- `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11805825/`

Supported proposition:

In the studied public-goods setting, participants engaged in costly punishment of defectors and punishment was associated with cooperation.

Bounded use:

Supports enforcement as a possible coordination mechanism. Does not establish that punishment is universally legitimate or monotonically beneficial.

### Boyd, Richerson, and Henrich — cultural adaptation and cooperation

Citation:

Robert Boyd, Peter J. Richerson, and Joseph Henrich, “Rapid Cultural Adaptation Can Facilitate the Evolution of Large-Scale Cooperation,” *Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology* 65 (2011): 431–444.

DOI:

`10.1007/s00265-010-1100-3`

Verified sources:

- `https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-010-1100-3`
- `https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3038225/`

Supported proposition:

Specific cultural transmission and norm-enforcement mechanisms can facilitate large-scale cooperation under stated model assumptions.

Bounded use:

Supports cultural adaptation as a candidate coordination mechanism and explicitly preserves the paper's qualification that cultural transmission does not always facilitate cooperation.

### Dunbar — group-size constraints

Citation:

R. I. M. Dunbar, “Neocortex Size as a Constraint on Group Size in Primates,” *Journal of Human Evolution* 22, no. 6 (1992): 469–493.

DOI:

`10.1016/0047-2484(92)90081-J`

Verified source:

`https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/004724849290081J`

Supported proposition:

The paper reports a relationship between relative neocortex volume and characteristic primate group size and interprets this as a constraint on simultaneously maintained social relationships.

Bounded use:

Supports group size as a candidate coordination parameter. Does not authorize treating the popularized human value near 150 as an exact universal invariant.
