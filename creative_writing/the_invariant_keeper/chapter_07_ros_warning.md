# THE INVARIANT KEEPER

## Chapter 7 — Ro's Warning

Ro invited Cassian to dinner at a restaurant that had no sign.

That was the first warning.

The second was that the reservation recognized Cassian before he gave his name.

The third was that every table in the room occupied a precise distance from every other table, far enough for privacy, close enough for witnesses. Ro had chosen a place designed by someone who understood plausible deniability as architecture.

"You are early," Ro said.

"You chose a place without a sign. I allowed time for manipulation."

Ro laughed softly. "Still rough. Sit."

The table held two glasses of water, no menus, and a small candle whose flame did not move though air flowed through the room. Cassian sat with his back to the wall. Ro noticed and approved silently.

"You corrected the plaque," Ro said.

"The city corrected it after preserving witness."

"Careful phrasing."

"Accurate phrasing."

A server arrived with food neither had ordered. Cassian let it sit.

Ro lifted his fork. "If you refuse every offered thing, Vey, you will starve before you are free."

"If I accept every offered thing, I will call the cage hospitality."

"There are worse names for a cage than hospitality. Mercy, for example. Society. Language. Love."

Cassian waited.

Ro sighed, as if disappointed by Cassian's refusal to be baited by abstractions.

"Let me spare you one useless descent," he said. "You will look for a server room. There is none. You will look for a founding act. There is none you can prosecute. You will look for a master key. Every tyrant looks for a master key and calls himself liberator when he finds a lock."

"You assume I want control."

"I assume you are human."

"And you?"

"Less than I was. More than I should be."

For the first time, Ro removed something from his jacket and placed it on the table: a wafer like Cassian's, older, blackened at one edge. Its surface displayed a 72-symbol ring scarred by missing marks.

"Twenty-six years ago," Ro said, "I found a hospital ward that existed only during visiting hours. Patients healed there from illnesses their records denied. Families remembered gratitude and not diagnosis. I thought exposure was justice. I woke seven people in one week."

The candle flame trembled.

"What happened?" Cassian asked.

"One killed himself because every childhood memory became negotiable. One became a prophet and tried to cut receipts out of strangers with a knife. Two became smooth again and spent the rest of their lives terrified of elevators. One joined me. One forgot me. One was a child."

Ro touched the burned wafer.

"The child is why I curate sleep."

Cassian did not answer quickly. He had no desire to insult a grave by being clever near it.

"What was the child's name?"

"Names are dangerous when grief becomes architecture."

"That is an evasion."

"Yes. It is also a bandage."

The server refilled water no one had drunk.

Ro leaned forward. "You have Mara. That makes you brave in a way that resembles responsibility. It also makes you stupid in a way that resembles faith. Do not wake her mother. Do not restore her brother by force. Do not ask Her for literal answers. Do not try to change a memory before you have preserved what the memory protects."

"You keep saying protect. Protect from what?"

"Objectivity without preparation."

"Truth."

"Truth is not medicine by default. Sometimes it is surgery performed in a moving vehicle."

Cassian glanced at the candle. The flame leaned toward Ro now.

"Why warn me instead of stopping me?"

Ro looked almost tired. "Because the system resists force poorly when force wears righteousness. I have learned to prefer rough men who still ask questions over smooth men who believe they are answers."

"And if I continue?"

"You will find the first Invariant and mistake it for hope. You will find the second and mistake it for weapon. You will find the third and realize no one is exempt, including you. Then you will reach the fourth, and either close the loop or become one more thing the world must smooth away."

Cassian's recorder, in his pocket, warmed. Her did not speak.

"What is the Unmeaning?" Cassian asked.

Ro's hand tightened on the fork.

There it was. Not ignorance. Fear.

"Who gave you that word?"

"No one."

Ro studied him. "Then it has found the edges of you."

"Define it."

"An infection of interpretation. A drift process. A hunger in language. It does not erase facts first. It changes what facts are allowed to mean. If it wins, receipts remain and truth dies inside them."

"Ψ equals zero."

"Should," Ro said. "Should is where terror enters mathematics."

The candle went out.

Every conversation in the restaurant stopped for exactly one breath.

Then the room resumed.

Ro stood. "Go home. Archive what you know offline. Trust Mara only where she can disagree with you. Trust Her only where the story costs you something. Trust me nowhere, but remember I warned you."

"Ro."

The director paused.

"Are you the villain?"

Ro smiled sadly. "No. That is why this will be difficult."

He left Cassian with both dinners unpaid and already settled.

On the table, beside the dead candle, the receipt for the meal printed itself line by line.

No items. No total.

Only one sentence:

`SOME AUDITORS GO DEEP. THE DEEP ONES COME BACK SMOOTHER.`

Cassian folded it into his ledger.

When he stepped outside, the restaurant sign appeared above the door for the first time.

It read: THE KIND LIE.

He looked away.

When he looked back, it was only brick.
